Myntra, Building Smarter Sourcing Intelligence

Myntra, FWD / product management internship / ~2 months / fashion commerce

A costing question should not need a relay through three teams. I worked across Design, Sourcing and Category Management to turn fragmented quotations and early price estimates into a shared decision-making workflow: T.H.R.E.A.D.

Context

Speed needs a shared view.

Within FWD’s fast-fashion workflow, a design must become a garment specification, a costed vendor quote and a commercially viable buying decision. A techpack describes how a garment should be made; its bill of materials (BOM) breaks that garment into cost components.

The internship report describes a recurring gap between those steps: the information needed for a decision existed, but was dispersed across people and files. Improving access to that information was a more practical intervention than adding another standalone analysis.

Design

Needs an early, explainable price baseline while refining a techpack, without losing creative time to cost follow-ups.

Sourcing

Needs comparable vendor quotations, clear approval status and awareness of price changes, so time can go into negotiation.

Category Management

Needs consolidated views of cost, selling price and margin by brand, article type and gender to review commercial trade-offs.

Discovery & priorities

Solve the handoffs before adding more analysis.

Working sessions with departmental leads and observation of their actual files surfaced four linked problems: quotations scattered across communications; slow collection and comparison; designers using ballpark prices without easy access to rates; and category reviews relying on manual exports. Price changes also needed to be visible and traceable.

The decision: extend a useful foundation.

The report documents early proposals for a pre-costed fabric programme, reusable garment blocks and standard techpack templates. Leadership instead approved extending an earlier intern’s costing prototype into a unified dashboard. This kept the intervention within the internship window and the team’s existing Google Sheets workflow.

Solution

Three entry points. One shared source.

A / Vendor quote comparison

Vendors enter quotations into standardized linked sheets. A sync consolidates them into the master workbook. The BOM viewer supports browsing by style, by vendor and across vendors, reducing the need to reconcile multiple files manually.

Coordinator approval actions include approve, reject with a target price, or drop. Row protections and change history connect the decision to the quotation. These controls support data integrity; they are not a claim that operational errors were eliminated.

B / AI-assisted cost estimation

A designer can use a techpack image to populate costing inputs or request a line-item cost estimate. Rate cards and similar approved historical costings provide anchors, while the user can inspect and edit the breakdown. The estimate supports an early design conversation; it does not replace vendor negotiation.

C / Category metrics

Summary and Charts views consolidate cost, selling-price and margin information by brand, article type and gender. Vendor and price-band views help teams inspect distributions rather than relying only on a single average. Quote status remains available in the BOM workflow, and freshness depends on the latest synchronization.

AI & trust

Show where each estimate comes from.

The report describes source tags for each cost line: a rate-card match, a historical neighbour, or an estimate requiring verification. Low-confidence extracted fields are highlighted for review. This makes uncertainty visible at the point where a person decides whether to trust a number.

Rate

Anchored to an official rate-card item. Check that the specification matches.

Neighbour

Anchored to a similar approved costing. Check whether the past garment is genuinely comparable.

Estimate

A fallback when grounding is weak. Treat it as a prompt for verification.

Similarity to earlier garments is documented. A separate recency-weighting algorithm is not established in the reviewed material, so it is not claimed here. Confidence signals are review aids, not independently validated accuracy scores.

Ownership

My work, within a broader team.

I mapped stakeholder workflows, translated the friction into product requirements, and developed the dashboard using Google Sheets, Apps Script and a web interface. The report describes iterative demos and feedback shaping the approval, history and analytics features, followed by user documentation for handover.

I built on a previous intern’s garment-cost calculator. Leadership approved the direction; Design, Sourcing and Category colleagues supplied domain knowledge, reviewed the workflows and provided feedback. The product was my implementation contribution within that shared operating context, rather than a claim of sole ownership of the underlying costing practice.

Time returned to the design cycle.

Reflection

A shared workflow is also a trust product.

Documented trade-offs

Extending the existing Sheets-based tool reduced the change required of users and made the project achievable. It also concentrated dependency on the master workbook, data quality and synchronization. Access controls, approvals and history were part of the product response. Vendors were not interviewed directly during discovery, which limits what can be claimed about their experience.

What I learned

  • Look at the files people use, not just the process they describe. The handoff can be the actual problem.
  • Reuse a working foundation when it preserves user familiarity and leaves more time for the highest-friction gaps.
  • Make AI uncertainty actionable: users need to know which cost lines require a check.
  • Design handover alongside features. A workflow tool needs to remain maintainable after its builder leaves.

Potential next improvements, not delivered claims

Run a structured pilot with a defined time-saved baseline; evaluate estimate quality by category and source type; research the vendor experience directly; and monitor sync freshness and failed updates. These would help distinguish a useful capability from a reliably sustained outcome.
